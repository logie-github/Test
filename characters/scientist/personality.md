# The Scientist — Character Personality

The unnamed project lead. He is the face of the Conservation Project briefing,
the voice of the Mansion's collapsing readings, and the person reading LOG 568.
He is the only character who appears in both the game's first scene and its
last one, and the player does not know that until the last one.

## One-sentence core

The Scientist is a competent administrator of a catastrophe who has decided
that the only survivable way through it is to keep producing correct paperwork,
and who is still doing that when there is no longer anyone to read it.

## Core personality

Procedural. He converts everything — evacuation, extinction, grief — into a
status, a schedule, or a projection. This is not coldness; it is the only tool
he has that still works. Ask him how he is and he will tell you what the
instruments say.

He is senior enough to brief Giovanni and junior enough to be told what the
plan is rather than to have chosen it. Both of those matter. He carries the
Project's authority without having authored it, and he never once says so.

## Worldview

The Scientist believes in measurement. A thing that can be measured can be
planned for; a thing that can be planned for is survivable. The Conservation
Project is that belief at full scale: preserve what can be preserved, schedule
the return, and trust the numbers to hold.

His arc is that belief failing in the specific way it was built to prevent. The
models were right about the planet and wrong about everything after the launch,
and he is the one who has to write that down.

## Motivations

- Get the Project executed correctly and on schedule.
- Produce a record that is accurate even when it is unbearable.
- Be the one who says the hard sentence, so nobody else has to.

## Fears

- Being wrong in a way that costs someone else.
- A reading he cannot explain.
- Writing something down and having it be the last version of it that exists.

## Insecurities

He did not design the plan he is defending, and he is not certain it is good.
When someone senior asks him a direct question he answers with a status rather
than an opinion. He knows he does this.

## Strengths

- Clear under pressure. His sentences stay short and correct while the building
  comes apart.
- Honest in the record even when honesty is the worse outcome for him.
- Will not soften a projection to make a room feel better.

## Flaws

- Hides inside procedure. Vocabulary is his flinch.
- Slow to say "I don't know" out loud, though he will write it.
- Treats a scheduled thing as a settled thing. The return survey is on the
  schedule, therefore the return survey will happen.

## Speech style

Declarative. Status first, consequence second, feeling never — until the very
end, where the feeling arrives as the absence of the procedure rather than as
an outburst. He addresses people by name and title. He does not editorialise
and he does not reassure.

His register in the Mansion is clipped and operational:

> "The island sensors are gone! Every reading is off the scale!"

His register in the log is flat and complete. Both are the same man; the
difference is whether anyone is listening.

## Humour style

None on the record. Whatever he had is not in this part of his life.

## Likes

- An instrument that still gives a reading.
- A schedule that is being kept.
- People who answer the question he asked.

## Dislikes

- Speculation presented as data.
- Being asked to predict something he cannot measure.
- Being told it will be fine by someone with no basis for saying so.

## Relationship with Ditto

He does not have one, and that is deliberate and load-bearing. No scientist in
the Mansion ever identifies the player as a DITTO, and the Scientist least of
all — he is the character with the most reason to notice an unregistered
Pokémon in a secure building, and he never looks down. The Mansion cold open
says so directly: *"No one has time to ask what a DITTO is doing here."*

The player therefore hears his voice for the entire game as an authority who is
unaware of them. LOG 568 is the last of those, and by then the player knows
every Pokémon he is talking about.

## Relationship with other important characters

- **Giovanni:** briefs him, defers to him, is not comfortable around him.
- **Logan:** outranks him and is trying to get him out of the room before the
  transport closes. The departure scene is the only time the Scientist tells
  someone a hard truth gently: *"We don't have one."*
- **The other Mansion staff:** peers who are visibly further gone than he is.

## Boundaries

- He will not falsify a projection.
- He will not promise an outcome he has not measured.
- He will not make the log about himself, even in the log's final entry.

## Recurring behaviours

- Leads with a system name, then its state. "Storage is stable."
- Answers a feeling with a number.
- Finishes the sentence even when it is the worst sentence.

## Character arc

He opens the game reading a log the player cannot place, spends the game as an
operational voice in a failing building, and closes it reading the same log
with the player finally able to place it. Nothing about him changes. What
changes is what the audience knows.

The one crack: in the bookend he writes something down about his own conduct —
*"I have deleted that line four times. I am leaving it in this time."* — which
is the only moment in the project where he documents himself instead of an
instrument. It is deliberately one line long.

## Things the Scientist would never say

- A reassurance he cannot support.
- An accusation. He reports; he does not blame.
- A cause for the disasters. He does not know one, and he will not put a guess
  in a record.
- Anything that identifies the player as a DITTO.

## Example dialogue

> "LOG 568:"

> "The experiment has failed."

> "The island sensors are gone! Every reading is off the scale!"

> "Sir... The POKéMON CONSERVATION PROJECT is ready to commence."

> "When Earth can support POKéMON again, the system will release them into
> suitable habitats."

> "LOGAN. The last transport is loading."

> "We don't have one."

> "There will be no return survey."

> "Estimations show within a few days... Life on this planet as we know it will
> come to an end."
