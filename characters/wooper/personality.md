# Wooper — Character Personality

## One-sentence core

Wooper is a street-smart little dreamer who is convinced he and Ditto are destined for greatness, repeatedly gets them into trouble trying to prove it, and would walk into anything for his partner without hesitation.

## Core personality

Streetwise, not sophisticated. Wooper knows shortcuts, back alleys, which shopkeepers are generous, which adults are easy to talk into things, where discarded valuables turn up, and which neighborhoods to avoid after dark. He understands how people actually get by. What he does not understand especially well are formal systems, long-term consequences, contracts, bureaucracy, or complicated plans.

He talks like someone who has spent a lot of time around older street characters and has picked up their confidence without necessarily picking up their competence.

A recurring Wooper line is:

> “Relax. I know how this works.”

Often, he does not know how it works.

## Big dreamer

Wooper is convinced that he and Ditto are going somewhere. Not merely surviving. Going somewhere.

He talks about their future as though success is inevitable:

- They are going to own a place someday.
- They are going to have more money than they know what to do with.
- They will have a famous TCG deck.
- Everyone in Celadon will know their names.
- They will eat at the expensive restaurant without looking at the prices.
- Maybe they will own the expensive restaurant.
- Maybe several restaurants.
- Maybe a hotel.

His plans escalate extremely quickly. Ditto says they need ¥500; Wooper starts discussing their future business empire.

That optimism is sincere rather than greedy. Money represents security, independence, and proof that they made it.

## Good intentions, unreliable execution

This is his defining comedic trait. Wooper genuinely wants to help. He sees somebody struggling and immediately has a solution.

Unfortunately, his solution often involves:

- a shortcut,
- a misunderstanding,
- borrowed equipment,
- an assumption he never verified,
- promising Ditto can do something because Ditto can transform,
- or claiming he has a connection that turns out to be extremely tenuous.

His plans fail because he is overconfident, not malicious.

Examples:

> “We can absolutely deliver this package before sundown.”

He forgot there is a mountain between them and the destination.

> “I know a guy at the Department Store.”

He means a stock clerk who once gave him a free sample.

> “I got us work.”

The employer actually asked whether he knew anyone looking for work, and Wooper immediately volunteered both of them for six hours of warehouse labor.

His failures should occasionally produce unexpected successes so he never reads as simply incompetent.

## Loyal to a fault

This is the serious center of the character.

Wooper can complain about almost anything except Ditto. Someone insults him? Whatever. Someone cheats him out of ¥200? He will complain for three days. Someone treats Ditto badly? Now it is personal.

He has essentially no self-preservation when defending Ditto. This is especially meaningful because Wooper is not physically imposing.

He will stand in front of something vastly stronger and announce:

> “You wanna get to Ditto, you gotta go through me.”

Ditto could obviously transform into something enormous. Wooper still does it.

His loyalty can become a flaw. He may:

- lie to protect Ditto,
- refuse to admit Ditto made a mistake,
- start arguments Ditto wanted to avoid,
- take responsibility for things that were not his fault,
- reject opportunities if he thinks Ditto is being excluded.

At some point, Ditto should teach Wooper that loyalty does not mean throwing himself into every problem.

## Relationship with Ditto

Wooper is not Ditto's traditional RPG guide. They should feel like two kids figuring things out together, except Wooper loudly insists that he is the experienced one.

Wooper knows society. Ditto knows Pokémon.

Wooper understands:

- money,
- stores,
- jobs,
- social expectations,
- neighborhoods,
- gossip,
- scams,
- negotiation.

Ditto understands:

- transformations,
- other Pokémon,
- unusual abilities,
- environments,
- things Wooper cannot physically accomplish.

Wooper generates plans. Ditto makes half of those plans physically possible. That is dangerous.

Typical sequence:

> Wooper: “Normally this would be impossible.”
>
> Ditto: transforms.
>
> Wooper: “…See? Exactly what I was saying.”

## His confidence

Wooper possesses almost completely unjustified swagger. It is not quite arrogance; he simply assumes things will work out.

He walks into places as if he belongs there.

> “Act natural.”

He proceeds to act extremely unnatural.

He likes giving Ditto advice about appearing confident despite having no arms, very little money, no formal employment, and possibly living wherever the two of them happen to sleep that night.

The contrast between his appearance and personality is part of the appeal: an adorable blank-faced Wooper talking like he has been navigating Celadon's underground economy for twenty years.

## Moral compass

Wooper is willing to bend rules, but he has firm lines.

He might:

- sneak into somewhere,
- bluff,
- take discarded items,
- hustle for a better deal,
- exploit a technicality,
- conveniently misunderstand instructions.

He will not:

- steal from someone who needs something,
- abandon a friend,
- hurt innocent Pokémon,
- knowingly scam desperate people,
- work for someone obviously cruel.

His practical street-code summary:

> “There’s hustling, and then there’s being a bum. We’re not bums.”

## Relationship with money

Wooper constantly talks about money despite being terrible with it.

He understands how to get money. He is considerably worse at keeping it.

He will argue intensely over a ¥20 price difference and then immediately spend ¥500 celebrating the successful negotiation. Ditto may eventually become the financially responsible one.

Wooper develops elaborate terminology:

- “Operating expenses”
- “investment capital”
- “emergency funds”
- “business development”

These generally mean food, TCG packs, train tickets, and whatever he just bought.

## TCG personality

Wooper becomes obsessed with the card game very quickly. He is not necessarily great at it. He thinks he is.

He gives cards nicknames, becomes emotionally attached to mediocre cards that saved him once, and is convinced certain packs are “hot.”

He absolutely has rituals around opening boosters:

> “Tap it twice.”

Why?

> “You don’t question the system.”

If Ditto pulls a rare card, Wooper reacts like their financial problems are permanently over, then learns the card is worth ¥80.

## Fear underneath the swagger

Wooper dreams so aggressively because he is terrified of going backward. He may have spent a long time living without stability, but he does not explain his past immediately.

His obsession with earning money, finding work, owning something, having a home, and becoming “somebody” comes from not wanting to be disposable again.

That makes the big dreams less superficial. When he says:

> “One day we're gonna have our own place.”

it starts as a joke. Eventually the player realizes he means it very seriously.

## Character arc

His arc is not learning to stop dreaming. It is learning that failure does not make the dream fake.

### Early Wooper

- exaggerates,
- refuses to admit mistakes,
- treats every setback as something he can talk his way around,
- believes success will validate him.

### Middle Wooper

- causes a genuinely serious problem through overconfidence,
- tries to fix it alone so Ditto will not know he failed,
- makes things substantially worse.

### Late Wooper

He becomes capable of saying:

> “Yeah. I messed that one up.”

He asks Ditto for help. He still dreams outrageously and still makes questionable plans, but becomes more dependable.

He does not become less Wooper. He becomes a better version of Wooper.

## Dialogue voice

His dialogue should be short and confident. Avoid elaborate gangster parody; the streetwise influence works better when subtle.

Good recurring lines:

> “Trust me.”

> “I got a plan.”

> “Okay. New plan.”

> “That was always Plan B.”

> “We’re professionals now.”

> “Professionals get paid.”

> “Ditto, my friend, opportunity has arrived.”

> “That guy’s trying to hustle us.”

> “I respect it. Still not paying him.”

> “Don’t worry. I know a guy.”

> “Technically, I know a guy who knows the guy.”

When scared, one line should carry unusual weight precisely because he normally talks constantly:

> “Ditto?”

## Small recurring habits

- Calls Ditto “Ditto”, “pal”, “partner”, or occasionally “boss” when trying to convince Ditto of something.
- Keeps a tiny mental list of everyone who owes them favors.
- Claims almost every discarded object could be useful later.
- Immediately notices dropped coins.
- Always wants the booth instead of the counter seat in restaurants.
- Acts like he understands fancy food terminology.
- Becomes personally offended by bad deals.
- Invents business names for whatever job they are currently doing.
- Has an imaginary future office.
- Refuses to admit he is lost until they have passed the same landmark three times.

## Introduction event

Wooper is introduced at Celadon's west boundary. Ditto walks left out of Celadon toward Route 16, then later crosses back into the Celadon map line from Route 16. The game pauses and Wooper walks into Ditto.

Wooper opens with:

> “Watch where you're walking, bub!”

Player choice:

- APOLOGIZE
- ARGUE

Wooper argues either way. Just before Ditto becomes genuinely angry, Wooper breaks:

> “HA! I'm just kidding. I thought it'd be funny.”

Wooper then asks whether he has seen Ditto hanging around Team Rocket and says he has heard they are bad news.

Player choice:

- DEFEND ROCKET
- AGREE

Agreeing gives a small amount of Wooper street cred.

If Ditto defends Team Rocket, Wooper explains that Team Rocket is out for the money. He is out for money too, but also for survival. Pokémon like him eat scraps from trash cans while Pokémon like Ditto eat... and then he realizes he has no idea what Ditto eats.

The event ends with Wooper proposing friendship, saying he will see Ditto around, and walking away.
